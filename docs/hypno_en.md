🧠 Hypnosis

**The Ultimate A²⁴ Guide**

21 Questions and Answers About Hypnosis


The source explains that hypnosis has been sought for various therapeutic purposes, including insomnia, nail biting, smoking dependence, stuttering, and bed-wetting. It also describes its use in attempts to identify people believed to have committed crimes.

It argues that scientific evidence and information about hypnosis should be presented so that it is not misunderstood as witchcraft, magic, or occult practice.

The following 21 questions and answers are provided so readers can compare them with what they already know.


====
1. Is Hypnosis a New Science?
====

**Answer**

The source states that hypnosis is not new. It describes evidence of its use in Egypt more than five thousand years ago, around 3000 BC, and says that Mesmer’s efforts in the 18th century helped the world recognize the field as a science.



====
2. Can Hypnosis Relieve or Eliminate Physical Pain?
====

**Answer**

The source says it can be used to relieve or eliminate pain. It gives historical examples involving Dr. Ward and Dr. Esdaille and notes that hypnosis has also been tested and used in connection with pain-free childbirth.



====
3. What Is Post-Hypnotic Suggestion?
====

**Answer**

The source explains that hypnotic suggestion means giving suggestions while a person is hypnotized. Post-hypnotic suggestion refers to instructions given during hypnosis that are later acted upon after the person wakes. An example given is suggesting that a smoker will no longer want cigarettes and will feel dislike toward them.



====
4. Can Hypnosis Be Used to Stop Alcohol and Cigarette Dependence?
====

**Answer**

The source says it can be used to help with alcohol and cigarette dependence and even describes its use in connection with drug dependence. It emphasizes that the practitioner’s skill and individualized suggestions are important, and that the same words cannot be used effectively for every person.



====
5. Can Hypnosis Make a Person Exceptionally Strong?
====

**Answer**

The source says this may be possible to some extent. It describes an experiment by Prof. Wesley R. Wells in which a hypnotized student reportedly maintained her arms in a rigid extended position for a much longer period than before hypnosis. It also gives the author’s own examples of people maintaining fixed arm positions and gripping objects strongly while hypnotized.



====
6. Is It Dangerous If a Hypnotized Person Cannot Be Awakened?
====

**Answer**

The source says there is no need for concern in the situation described. It states that if the hypnotist leaves unexpectedly, or even dies, the hypnotized person will eventually return to a normal waking state when the appropriate time comes.



====
7. Can Hypnosis Be Dangerous for Other Reasons?
====

**Answer**

The source compares the risks of hypnosis in skilled hands with other skilled professional procedures. It warns that inexperienced experimentation may lead to unexpected psychological problems and says hypnosis should be learned carefully from a properly trained and experienced practitioner.



====
8. Can Amnesia Be Effectively Treated with Hypnosis?
====

**Answer**

The source says it can be used to treat amnesia and describes hypnosis as a direct and effective way of helping bring forgotten memories back. It gives examples involving names, homes, relatives, childhood experiences, focused attention, writing, and guided recall.



====
9. Can a Person Temporarily Return to Childhood?
====

**Answer**

The source says a person can temporarily return to an earlier stage of life in hypnotic experiments. It gives an example of a person who once spoke Polish as a child and reportedly spoke it again under hypnosis, and discusses using childhood experiences in work with people who stutter.



====
10. Can Hypnosis Reveal a Person’s Inner Attitudes Toward Parents, Children, and Friends?
====

**Answer**

The source says a person’s attitudes toward family members can be brought out under hypnosis. It describes a case in which a girl was asked to describe how she viewed her father while hypnotized, and the source interprets her responses as expressions of attitudes held in her inner mind.



====
11. Can Everyone Be Hypnotized?
====

**Answer**

The source explains that hypnosis has different levels. It says only a portion of people reach a very deep level, while moderate and light levels are much more widely attainable. It describes differences in awareness, memory, and response at these levels.



====
12. Are Children Easier to Hypnotize Than Adults?
====

**Answer**

The source says children are easier to bring into a deep hypnotic state than older adults, giving figures of about 50 percent for children aged five to seven and about 7 percent for adults aged 50 to 65.



====
13. Can Someone Who Does Not Want to Be Hypnotized Be Hypnotized?
====

**Answer**

The source says a person who does not want to be hypnotized cannot be hypnotized in the way described. Cooperation and willingness are presented as necessary. It also gives examples of people who agreed to try and consequently became cooperative participants.



====
14. Are Older People the Easiest People to Hypnotize?
====

**Answer**

The source says no. It describes the idea that older people are easiest to hypnotize as the opposite of the truth.



====
15. Are Women Easier to Hypnotize?
====

**Answer**

The source states that sex, ethnicity, nationality, and similar factors are not related to how easy hypnosis is.



====
16. Can Hypnosis Be Used to Improve a Person’s Memory?
====

**Answer**

The source says hypnosis may improve memory when used with meaningful poems, literature, and similar material, or when specifically used to improve memory. It says no obvious improvement was observed for meaningless or arbitrary combinations of letters.



====
17. What Is Self-Hypnosis?
====

**Answer**

The source explains that definitions of self-hypnosis differ. It discusses auto-hypnosis and auto-suggestion and gives an example in which a person is first hypnotized and later uses a learned suggestion to reduce pain. It says the topic will be discussed more fully later in the book.



====
18. Is Hypnosis a Type of Sleep?
====

**Answer**

The source says hypnosis is not the same as sleep. It compares breathing, pulse, brain-wave patterns, and responsiveness, noting that a hypnotized person can hear speech and follow instructions while a sleeping person cannot normally do so.



====
19. Can a Person Be Hypnotized Within Seconds?
====

**Answer**

The source says the speed of hypnosis varies from person to person. It states that an ordinary person is not normally hypnotized within seconds, while stage performers may appear to do so because post-hypnotic suggestions were given beforehand. The author describes typical times ranging from about five minutes to half an hour, with some people responding in around thirty seconds.



====
20. Can Hypnosis Help a Person Who Stutters?
====

**Answer**

The source says hypnosis can help some people who stutter. It emphasizes individualized suggestions and describes both single-session and longer treatments. It mentions possible factors such as breathing, muscle tension, embarrassment, and fear.



====
21. How Long Can a Suggestion Given During Hypnosis Last?
====

**Answer**

The source cites Dr. Patton as reporting that a post-hypnotic suggestion lasted eight months and Dr. Wells as saying it could remain fully effective for one year. It concludes that such suggestions do not last forever and says renewed suggestions may be recommended about once a year for some treated problems.
